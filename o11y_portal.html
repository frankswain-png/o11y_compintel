ClickHouse

Active

ClickHouse used dbt Summit in Las Vegas (15–18 September) to publish two connected announcements on 16 September. The ClickHouse adapter for dbt v2 — a ground-up Rust rewrite of dbt — reached public beta, shipping inside the dbt binary and connecting over the ClickHouse ADBC driver rather than as a separate Python package; dbt Labs reports parse times up to 30x faster than dbt v1. Separately, ClickHouse became the first partner-built v2 adapter available on the dbt platform itself, in private beta, letting teams develop ClickHouse models in dbt's Studio IDE, run them on scheduled jobs, and browse them in Catalog. dbt Labs VP of Worldwide Partner Ecosystem Shawn Toldo was quoted framing the pairing around agentic trust: "the companies that win with AI will be the ones whose agents can be trusted with the numbers."

On 17 September, ClickHouse also shipped an official Apache Airflow provider, apache-airflow-providers-clickhousedb, now upstream in the Airflow project and built on the in-house clickhouse-connect Python client. It replaces a community plugin that contributor Anton Bryzgalov had maintained for years as the de facto standard, and gives Airflow's standard SQL operator and a new ClickHouse hook direct, bulk-insert-capable access — the same pattern ClickHouse said it runs internally, scheduling roughly 6 billion rows a day across 76 DAGs.

Neither announcement is an observability feature on its own, but both extend ClickHouse's reach into the orchestration and transformation tooling that surrounds telemetry pipelines, at the same time ClickHouse continues to sell ClickStack, its OpenTelemetry-native observability stack, directly against Elastic.

Watch signal: ClickHouse is building adjacency into the data-engineering stack — dbt, Airflow, and Postgres via WalShadow — around its core database, which widens the surface area where a ClickHouse-plus-ClickStack pitch can displace a broader Elastic Stack deployment beyond pure log and metric storage.

Standing items: the v26.9 community release call took place on 17 September, within this window. ClickHouse's CostBench performance-per-dollar campaign remains paused after the 8 September Snowflake teardown — no BigQuery or Redshift instalment has followed, so the methodology risk to observability comparisons stays live but dormant. Commercial standing is $250 million ARR and more than 4,000 customers as of 27 May, on a $15 billion valuation from the January Series D. RunReveal, a ClickHouse-based security analytics platform, was acquired 1 September for undisclosed terms; no product announcement has followed. On ClickStack, the observability product itself, the most recent update is an August recap published 16 September covering the prior month's feature work — no new ClickStack capability shipped in this window. The London User Conference is 30 September and Open House Bengaluru is 22 September.

Splunk / Cisco

Active

Cisco published a press release on 15 September, timed to .conf26 in Denver (14–17 September), that bundled the observability content of the conference into one announcement. Cisco AI POD for Splunk — the newest configuration in Cisco Secure AI Factory with NVIDIA — brings self-managed Splunk AI to on-premises, private-cloud and air-gapped Splunk Enterprise customers for the first time, running on NVIDIA-accelerated, Kubernetes-based infrastructure that Cisco says is pre-validated for Splunk AI workloads. Customers can self-host the Cisco Deep Time Series Model, Google Gemma 4 or OpenAI GPT-OSS 20B today, with NVIDIA Nemotron open models to follow; Splunk AI Assistant runs in agent mode now, with a no-code Agent Launchpad promised later this year. Cisco named channel partners Accenture, bitsIO, Wipro and World Wide Technology as ready to help customers stand up the AI POD on day one.

On the observability product side, Splunk Agent Observability — previously an on-premises-only offering — is now generally available in Splunk Observability Cloud and in Cisco Cloud Control, and ships with a new Tokenomics capability that tracks and forecasts AI token spend, including employee use of coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor, using the Cisco Deep Time Series Model to project consumption before a billing period ends. A new Observability Studio aims to make new applications "born observable" from development onward, and a new Network Intelligence App — reported in detail by Network World's Zeus Kerravala on 15 September — brings Cisco network topology, device health and event data into Splunk, extended by ThousandEyes-informed synthetic testing, so a network-versus-application dispute can start from correlated evidence rather than dashboard-switching. Two new Observability Cloud editions, Essentials and Premier, simplify how the product is bought and expanded.

The through-line across both the AI POD and the Tokenomics/Network Intelligence bundle is that Cisco is answering the two objections — data sovereignty and AI cost unpredictability — that have been the most quotable gaps in SaaS-only observability platforms this year, and it is doing so with shipped product timed to its own conference rather than roadmap promises. Splunk and AWS separately announced an expanded, multi-year strategic partnership around the Agentic SOC in the same window.

Watch signal: Splunk now has an on-premises, air-gapped answer to AI sovereignty and a metered answer to AI cost, which narrows two of the arguments Elastic has used against Splunk in regulated and cost-sensitive accounts; the counter-argument shifts toward what a single-vendor Cisco/NVIDIA appliance stack costs to run and how much genuine model choice it preserves.

Standing items: .conf26 ran 14–17 September in Denver, so this bundle covers the conference in full; the 2026 Splunkie Awards, presented during the keynote, went to Verizon, Global Emancipation Network, Trafikverket, UnitedHealth Group and Anthropic. Splunk Enterprise Security 8.7.0 shipped 2 September, before this window. The last formal Splunk press release before .conf26 was 19 May, underscoring how conference-concentrated Splunk's news cycle remains.
