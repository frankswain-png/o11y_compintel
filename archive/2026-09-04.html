Vendor details
Datadog Active

No product or partnership news, but the market story became the story. A 24/7 Wall St analysis published 2 September marked Datadog down 22% over the prior month, trading at $210.76 intraday and closing the session at $209.23, off 6.53% on the day. The decline traces to a single dated session: on 6 August the stock fell 19% after Q2 results that beat on revenue, beat on earnings, and came with raised full-year guidance. What broke the multiple was the disclosure that a very large AI customer would reduce usage going forward, folded into both Q3 and full-year guidance.

The isolation is what makes this readable rather than noise. Over the same month the First Trust Cloud Computing ETF rose 11%, CoreWeave gained 14%, Oracle gained 13%, and Cloudflare slipped only 3%. That rules out sector rotation and leaves a company-specific repricing on customer-concentration risk. Q2 revenue was $1.12 billion, up 36% year over year — the business is not the problem, the shape of forward growth is.

Watch signal: Datadog's AI-native revenue is now visibly concentrated, and a vendor repricing on renewal risk defends renewals harder. Expect more aggressive commercial terms in contested observability deals, and expect Datadog account teams to lean on platform breadth rather than usage growth. The counter-position for Elastic is predictable cost at volume, which is the exact axis Datadog customers are now being asked about by their own boards.

Standing items: Datadog presents at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology Conference on 10 September at 12:30pm ET, the first venue for updated AI-revenue commentary since the August guide. Product context out of window: DASH 2026 in June shipped more than 100 new capabilities and the company acquired Adaptive ML, a reinforcement-learning operations platform. Open question is whether the AI customer reduction is a one-off renewal or the first of several.

Grafana Labs Quiet

Nothing published in the 1–4 September window — no product, partnership or analyst news.

The cadence remains post-GrafanaCON quiet, five months after the April 2026 event where Grafana 13 launched with Git Sync generally available, Grafana Advisor health checks, saved queries, and Grafana Assistant extended from Cloud into OSS and Enterprise. The Loki work is the piece that matters competitively: a new query planner delivering up to 20x less data scanned and 10x faster aggregated queries, on top of the Kafka-backed ingestion rearchitecture. That is Grafana attacking the cost-per-query argument directly.

Grafana Labs was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms and positioned furthest in Completeness of Vision, announced 15 July 2026. The Grafana Marketplace remains in pilot and is still the open item worth tracking, since it is the mechanism by which Grafana converts its plugin ecosystem into a commercial distribution channel.

ClickHouse Active

ClickHouse published six posts across 1–3 September, and the split matters more than the volume. The observability-relevant three all landed on 1 September: a user story claiming Uken Games reduced observability costs by 87% by moving its stack to ClickHouse; an engineering post introducing an Agentic Analytics Benchmark for measuring model accuracy and efficiency in analytical agents; and the company post welcoming RunReveal. The Managed Postgres track carried the rest — Scheduled Upgrades for Scale and Enterprise users on 3 September, a Neon Postgres to ClickHouse Managed Postgres migration story and the MySQL CDC connector for ClickPipes reaching general availability on 2 September.

This corrects last cycle's read. The four-posts-in-four-days Managed Postgres run looked like a pivot away from observability; with the 1 September posts included, ClickHouse is running both tracks at once — operational database beneath, observability and agentic analytics above. The 87% figure is the sharper artifact. It is a named customer, a specific percentage and a published URL, which is exactly the form procurement teams cite, and it follows the 27 August engineering post asking whether ClickHouse is winning the observability wars.

Watch signal: ClickHouse has moved from architectural argument to quantified cost claim, and that changes where the competitive conversation starts. Expect the 87% number in renewal discussions where log retention volume is the contested line item. Elastic's answer needs to be a comparable named-customer economics story rather than a feature response, and the durable differentiators remain search, security and the unified platform that ClickStack does not cover.

Standing items: the RunReveal acquisition is still fresh and the security-data direction it implies has not yet produced a product announcement. Managed ClickStack and the Agentic Data Stack are both positioned as first-class offerings on the ClickHouse site, and the company runs a Stockholm in-person real-time analytics training on 23 September. Open question is whether the Postgres, CDC and observability tracks converge into a single stack pitch or stay separately sold.

Dynatrace Quiet

Nothing published in the 1–4 September window. The most recent items on the Dynatrace news centre are trade-press pieces dated 31 August, including CRN's analysis of the Arize deal, all now outside the window.

Two August items remain live. Dynatrace announced its intent to acquire AI observability vendor Arize on 13 August; the deal has not been reported as closed. On 17 August the company priced a private placement of $1.25 billion of exchangeable senior notes due 2031 through Dynatrace LLC, at a 0% coupon with an initial exchange price of approximately $64.27 per share — a 35% premium to the $47.61 close — settling 20 August for roughly $1.227 billion net, with about $134.7 million earmarked to repurchase 2.83 million shares. A zero-coupon raise of that size alongside an AI-observability acquisition reads as a company funding a buying position rather than a defensive one.

Dynatrace was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms for the sixteenth time, positioned highest for Ability to Execute, announced 15 July. The company participates in the Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology Conference on 9 September, the next likely venue for Arize integration and close commentary. Reported scale: approximately 4,100 global customers and 5,200-plus employees.

New Relic Quiet

Nothing published in the 1–4 September window, and the Japan region GA logged in the previous edition has now aged out with no follow-on announcement.

The 2026 arc is consistently AI-lifecycle rather than infrastructure. In February at New Relic Advance 2026 the company launched an SRE Agent and framed its direction as observability beyond human scale. In June it launched New Relic for Startups — free platform access including every AI feature — and New Relic AI Coding Observability, an open-source offering aimed at AI-assisted development lifecycles, with New Relic Autopilot slated for late July. It also published research finding that AI-generated code grades higher in review yet correlates with a rise in production incidents, which is the sharpest piece of demand-generation positioning the company has run this year.

The Microsoft relationship is the commercial engine to watch: at Build 2026 in June, New Relic reported strong double-digit year-over-year growth in committed bookings through the Azure Marketplace for the period ending 31 March 2026, alongside GitHub Copilot coding-agent integration and a Security RX integration for Copilot. Marketplace-led co-sell is the channel most likely to appear opposite Elastic in Azure-committed accounts.

Splunk / Cisco Quiet

Nothing published in the 1–4 September window — a fourth consecutive silent cycle, and the Splunk newsroom shows no press release since 19 May 2026.

Four months of press silence ten days out from the company's largest event reads as a pre-event embargo rather than an absence of news. .conf26 runs 14–17 September at the Colorado Convention Center in Denver, themed around digital resilience in the agentic era. The 2026 announcements should be read against what Cisco set up at .conf25: Splunk Agent Observability powered by Galileo, and the Cisco Data Fabric built on the Splunk Platform across data, context, action and governance layers, with core capabilities shipping and more rolling out through 2026. Cisco AI Canvas integration with Splunk Cloud Platform was scheduled for 2026 availability as part of Data Fabric.

The 2026 press record is thin but directionally consistent: agentic AI for CISOs in February, a TPG Telecom security and service operations win in Australia in March, agentic-workforce security in March, and $600 billion downtime research in May. Expect the next two pulses to be dominated by Splunk, and pre-brief field teams on Data Fabric and federated-search positioning before the announcements land.
