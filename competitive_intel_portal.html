Vendor details

Datadog

Active

Datadog's only new material in the window came from an investor stage rather than a product page. Co-founder and CEO Olivier Pomel appeared at Citi's 2026 Global TMT Conference on 8 September at 11:30am ET, in a keynote session with Fatima Boolani, who co-leads Citi's enterprise software coverage. The disclosed figures: revenue up 36% in the latest quarter, an acceleration on prior periods; non-AI customers growing in the high-20% range against roughly 18% a year ago; and an AI-native customer base of about 750, up from fewer than 100 two years ago. Pomel described observability as a core layer for AI systems, with monitoring, validation and cost control becoming more important, and said Datadog is moving toward AI credits and other flexible pricing models while gross retention stays in the high 90s. He also named rising competition, changing pricing models and higher infrastructure costs as pressures on the next phase. Shares traded between $209.00 and $225.96 on 9 September and closed at $224.63.

The broadening figure is the one that matters. The open question carried through the last several editions was whether Datadog's growth depends on a small set of AI-native accounts whose usage can be reduced in a single renewal; non-AI cohort growth moving from roughly 18% to the high 20s is the first public answer, and it argues that AI is lifting the base rather than concentrating in it. The pricing disclosure is the second-order read: a shift toward credits converts consumption risk into committed spend, which is a defensive move dressed as a packaging change, and it will show up in competitive deals as multi-year commitments rather than as list-price comparisons.

Watch signal: Credits reprice the conversation away from per-gigabyte ingest, which is where Elastic's cost argument is strongest. Where Datadog arrives with a committed-credit structure, the Elastic counter has to be measured over the life of the commitment — retention economics, what happens to unused capacity, and the cost of the data the customer chose not to send — rather than on unit price at the point of ingest.

Standing items: the Citi appearance is now past; Datadog presents at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology Conference today, 10 September, at 12:30pm ET, the next venue for updated AI-revenue commentary. Datadog Summit San Francisco runs 23 September at the Hyatt Regency, a free one-day event on observability, security and AI with engineers from Anthropic, Adobe and Cognition on the agenda. Product backdrop remains DASH 2026 in June, which shipped more than 100 capabilities including autonomous Bits AI, AI Guard, Bring Your Own Cloud, Bits Detection in preview and the Datadog Agent Console for tracking activity across Claude Code, Cursor and GitHub Copilot alongside Datadog's own agents; the company also acquired Adaptive ML. The open question is narrowed but not closed: whether the disclosed AI-customer usage reduction was a single renewal or the first of several.

ClickHouse

Active

ClickHouse published the first end-to-end results from CostBench on 8 September, authored by Tom Schreiber and Lionel Palacin. The benchmark streamed a 113.2-billion-row National Best Bid and Offer stock-quote dataset at a target of 1 million rows per second into ClickHouse Cloud, Snowflake, BigQuery and Redshift Serverless through each vendor's recommended low-latency ingest path, while four aggregate queries ran every ten minutes and two drill-down queries every hour throughout ingestion. The headline claim is a 412–1,996× end-to-end performance-per-dollar advantage for ClickHouse Cloud, against 32–101× when only the query side was measured in the earlier round. The underlying figures are specific: fresh-data-path cost of $28.69 for ClickHouse Cloud versus $79.42 for Snowflake over an approximately 37-hour run, with database storage excluded from the score on the stated grounds that it added roughly $0.50 and $0.81 respectively. A 1:1 Snowflake analysis followed on 10 September, the first in a promised series. Separately, David Wheeler introduced a chdb Postgres extension for high-performance imports from cloud storage on 8 September.

The argument being built is not about observability, and that is the point. CostBench targets cloud data warehouses, publishes its harness, queries, accepted runs and cost summaries in an open GitHub repository, and frames the whole comparison around the cost of keeping continuously arriving data query-ready — the exact cost structure that dominates log and telemetry workloads. ClickHouse already runs Elastic, Splunk and Datadog observability comparison pages using the same performance-per-dollar vocabulary. Publishing a reproducible warehouse benchmark first establishes the methodology as neutral, then lets the observability comparison inherit its credibility. The promised series of per-vendor teardowns also means this is a campaign with weeks of runway rather than a single post, and the open repository makes it awkward to dismiss without engaging the numbers.

Watch signal: The methodology, not the multiple, is what Elastic should read closely. CostBench scores fresh-data-path cost, normalised query-serving cost and accumulated query runtime, and explicitly excludes storage, free tiers, discounts and idle capacity — choices that flatter a system optimised for sustained ingest and disadvantage anything whose value shows up in retention, search relevance or cross-signal correlation. If this methodology is pointed at observability, Elastic's response needs to contest the boundary of what is being measured, and to move the comparison onto total cost across the full retention window with security and search in scope.

Standing items: publishing cadence remains high, with "ClickHouse as a streaming HTTP API" on 5 September, "How MCP Toolbox turns agent text into ClickHouse vectors" on 7 September, "Pipelined SQL in ClickHouse 26.8" and a real-time market data app walkthrough on 4 September, scheduled upgrades for Managed Postgres on 3 September, MySQL CDC for ClickPipes reaching general availability on 2 September, and the RunReveal team welcomed on 1 September — the security-data direction that implies has still produced no product announcement. Managed ClickStack reached general availability at Open House in late May 2026, priced on infrastructure rather than events at under three cents per gigabyte per month for full-fidelity OpenTelemetry data and $0.002 per gigabyte per month for long-term retention; the pricing model, not the product, is the competitive threat. The ClickStack UI has been embedded in the ClickHouse binary since version 26.2, adding roughly 4.2MB and making an observability UI available on any install, though it omits alerting and persistence and is explicitly not for production. Financial backdrop: a $400 million Series D led by Dragoneer closed 16 January 2026 at a $15 billion valuation, with annualised revenue past $250 million and ClickHouse Cloud past 4,000 customers as of the May 2026 disclosure. A hands-on session on building agents with ClickHouse and LibreChat runs in Boston on 14 September, and Open House Bengaluru is on 22 September with the top three Click-a-Thon teams presenting on the main stage.

Dynatrace

Active

Dynatrace appeared at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on 9 September, with CEO Rick McConnell and CFO Jim Benson presenting. The framing was two markets at once: traditional observability, and AI observability as a newer adjacent category. The figures given were record first-quarter new-logo ARR growth of 160%, organic net new ARR growth of 41%, and consumption growth running above 20%, with AI workloads said to use the platform at a 50% higher rate than non-AI customers. McConnell also acknowledged that current ARR growth remains below the pace the company expects from its next phase — an unusually direct admission for a conference appearance, and the detail most worth logging.

Read alongside Datadog the day before, the two presentations describe the same mechanism from different positions. Both argue AI is a consumption multiplier on an existing platform rather than a new product to be sold separately; Datadog is repackaging to capture it through credits, Dynatrace is relying on native consumption uplift. The gap between 160% new-logo ARR growth and ARR growth that management concedes is below target is the tell: Dynatrace is winning new accounts at pace while the installed base expands more slowly than the plan requires. That is the profile of a company whose land motion works and whose expand motion is under pressure — which is where an acquisitive posture, and the Arize deal, start to look less optional.

Watch signal: A company acquiring into AI observability while conceding base-growth pressure is a company that will compete hard on displacement in the next two quarters. Elastic should expect Dynatrace to lead with AI-workload observability in accounts where it is already deployed, and the ground where Elastic can take the argument is the cost of retaining the underlying telemetry at AI-era volumes, with search and security in the same platform, rather than on AI-monitoring features alone.

Standing items: the Arize acquisition remains the live corporate item and is not reported as closed. Dynatrace signed a definitive agreement on 13 August to acquire the AI observability vendor for approximately $915 million, about $815 million in cash plus replacement equity awards, expected to close later this quarter or early in Dynatrace's fiscal third quarter subject to regulatory review; the 17 August private placement of $1.25 billion of zero-coupon exchangeable senior notes due 2031 finances that posture. Bindplane was acquired in April 2026 to establish telemetry pipelines. The most recent press release remains "As AI scales across enterprises, breaking points emerge", dated 25 August. Dynatrace was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms for the sixteenth time, announced 15 July 2026, and announced its intent to pursue FedRAMP High on 2 July. On the community side, the September newsletter confirmed the Dynatrace Community will migrate from Khoros to Discourse with the opening targeted for the end of October 2026, and named the open-source dtwiz CLI — which inspects a host, ranks ingestion options and installs the right one, whether OpenTelemetry Collector, OneAgent, the Kubernetes Operator or a cloud integration — as its topic of the month. Dynatrace is a silver sponsor at WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America in San José from 23 to 25 September, at booth 337, demonstrating the onboarding experience including Bindplane. Reported scale is approximately 4,100 global customers.

Quiet

Splunk / Cisco

Quiet

Nothing was published in the 7–10 September window — a seventh consecutive silent cycle on the newsroom, which still shows no press release since 19 May 2026, with the most recent 2026 releases dating to 23 March on security for the agentic workforce and 4 March on TPG Telecom in Australia. The silence is almost certainly deliberate: .conf26 opens in four days and the announcement inventory is being held for it. Shipping has not stopped in the meantime — Splunk Enterprise Security 8.7.0 was released on 2 September 2026 and remains the most substantive Splunk artifact of the past month, moving the AI SOC Analyst from investigation to response by automatically executing permitted actions and recommending next steps from investigation context, and adding a Guided Response Agent, Automation and Detection Builder agents, MCP access to investigation context, refreshed navigation and deeper change history. Shipping an agent with permission to act, rather than one that only advises, is the claim the .conf26 security narrative will be built on. Running on a separate track is federated search in Splunk Cloud Platform, which queries Amazon S3 datasets — including those backed by an Iceberg REST catalog — without ingesting the data; it is Cloud Platform only, not available for Splunk Enterprise on-premises or BYOL, nor on GCP, FedRAMP High or IL5 stacks, so the ingest-economics argument still lands in on-premises and regulated estates while in Splunk Cloud accounts the contest moves to query performance over cold object storage and total platform cost. The catalyst is imminent: .conf26 runs 14–17 September at the Colorado Convention Center in Denver, with the welcome keynote on Monday 14 September from 5:00 to 6:20pm MT presented jointly by Cisco's Jeetu Patel and Splunk general manager Kamal Hathi. The agenda is organised around three pillars — Platform, framed as turning machine data into agentic action; Security, as defending at machine speed; and Observability, as optimising AI at scale — with Cisco Data Fabric and Federated Search for Snowflake flagged as headline items. This is the last pulse before the announcements land; brief field teams on Data Fabric and federated-search positioning now, and expect the next two editions to be Splunk-dominated.
