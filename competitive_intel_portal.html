TLDR

The window belongs to ClickHouse, which shipped four items in four days and converted its benchmark campaign into product and distribution: the private preview of On-Demand Compute on 10 September, a new distributed query execution framework and cost-based optimizer that let a single query lease workers from a shared pool rather than scaling the whole service; launch-partner status for the Data agent in ChatGPT Work the same day; the first end-to-end CostBench results on 8 September claiming a 412–1,996× performance-per-dollar advantage over Snowflake, BigQuery and Redshift Serverless on a 113.2-billion-row stream; and a 1:1 Snowflake teardown on 10 September. Vendor details

Datadog

Active

Datadog's single in-window item was an investor appearance. CFO David Obstler presented at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on 10 September at 12:30pm ET, two days after co-founder and CEO Olivier Pomel had appeared at Citi's Global TMT Conference on 8 September. The Goldman session was notable for what it emphasised: not the DASH feature inventory, but the observability dataset Datadog has accumulated since the company was founded, and the claim that aggregating signals across client-facing applications at that scale makes the platform predictive rather than merely diagnostic. Obstler connected this to the Adaptive ML acquisition, a reinforcement-learning team whose techniques Datadog intends to apply to IT management and observability problems.

The shift in argument is the part worth logging. For most of 2026 Datadog has competed on breadth — more than 100 capabilities shipped at DASH, an agent console, AI Guard, Bring Your Own Cloud. Pitching a proprietary training corpus instead is a different kind of claim, because it is not something a competitor closes by shipping features; it is defensible in proportion to how much customer telemetry sits inside Datadog and how long it stays there. That makes retention length and data gravity the strategic asset, and it quietly reframes every ingest-and-retain conversation as an investment in Datadog's model quality rather than the customer's.

Watch signal: A data-moat argument cuts both ways, and the counter is ownership. Where Datadog positions accumulated telemetry as the source of predictive advantage, Elastic's opening is that the customer keeps the data in an open format they can query, retain and move — the model improves on their corpus, not on someone else's. Field teams should expect "our AI is better because of our data" and should be ready to ask whose data it is.

Standing items: both September investor appearances are now past — Citi on 8 September, Goldman Sachs on 10 September. Datadog Summit San Francisco runs on 23 September at the Hyatt Regency, a free one-day event on observability, security and AI. Product backdrop remains DASH 2026 in June, which shipped more than 100 capabilities including autonomous Bits AI for end-to-end incident detection and remediation, AI Guard against prompt injection and poisoning, Bring Your Own Cloud, and Bits Agent Builder. From the Citi session on 8 September: revenue up 36% in the latest quarter, non-AI customers growing in the high-20% range against roughly 18% a year ago, an AI-native base of about 750 customers, gross retention in the high 90s, and a stated shift toward AI credits and more flexible pricing.

ClickHouse

Active

ClickHouse published four items inside the window, and the most consequential is the one that is not a benchmark. On 10 September Melvyn Peignon announced the private preview of ClickHouse On-Demand Compute, which lets an individual query execute on a shared pool of ClickHouse-managed workers outside the customer's own cluster. It is enabled by two query settings — make_distributed_plan and distributed_plan_workers_num — and rests on two new engine capabilities: a distributed query execution framework built on multistage query execution, and a cost-based optimizer that selects among execution plans. Workers are leased for a 60-second minimum, renew automatically while a query runs, and stay warm for reuse with no cold start until the lease expires; concurrent queries share a pool sized to the largest single request rather than the sum. It works over SharedMergeTree, Iceberg and Delta. ClickHouse's own benchmark on TPC-H SF10 reports the on-demand cluster matching a five-node autoscaling cluster at 25 concurrent queries while using 50% less compute, and notes that the autoscaler was still recommending 80 CPU cores hours after the burst ended.

The same day, ClickHouse announced it is a launch partner for the Data agent in ChatGPT Work, connecting ClickHouse Cloud to natural-language queries, reports and interactive dashboards, and published a 1:1 Snowflake teardown of the performance-per-dollar gap first quantified on 8 September. That 8 September post — CostBench's first end-to-end results, by Tom Schreiber and Lionel Palacin — streamed a 113.2-billion-row National Best Bid and Offer quote dataset at a target of 1 million rows per second into ClickHouse Cloud, Snowflake, BigQuery and Redshift Serverless while aggregate queries ran every ten minutes and drill-downs every hour, and reported a 412–1,996× end-to-end performance-per-dollar advantage against 32–101× when only query cost was counted. The specific figures: $28.69 fresh-data-path cost for ClickHouse Cloud against $79.42 for Snowflake over an approximately 37-hour run, with storage excluded on the grounds that it contributed roughly $0.50 and $0.81. Managed Postgres reached private preview on Google Cloud on 9 September.

Read together, these are two halves of one move. CostBench establishes, in an open and reproducible repository, that the cost of keeping continuously arriving data query-ready is the number that matters — which is precisely the cost structure of log and telemetry workloads, and precisely the frame ClickHouse already uses on its Elastic, Splunk and Datadog observability comparison pages. On-Demand Compute then makes that frame purchasable: pay for burst compute per query instead of provisioning for peak. The ChatGPT Work partnership adds a distribution channel that bypasses the observability UI question entirely. This is a company assembling a cost-and-access argument rather than a feature list.

Watch signal: The methodology is still what Elastic should contest, not the multiple. CostBench scores fresh-data-path cost, normalised query-serving cost and accumulated query runtime while explicitly excluding storage, free tiers, discounts and idle capacity — boundaries that flatter sustained-ingest systems and write out everything whose value appears in long retention, search relevance, cross-signal correlation and security analytics on the same data. On-Demand Compute sharpens the point by making burst query cost a line item a buyer can compare directly. The Elastic response is to move the comparison to total cost across the full retention window with search and security in scope, and to be explicit about what the benchmark declines to price.

Standing items: publishing cadence remains high, with the chdb Postgres extension for high-performance imports from cloud storage on 8 September, "How MCP Toolbox turns agent text into ClickHouse vectors" on 7 September, "ClickHouse as a streaming HTTP API" on 5 September, "Pipelined SQL in ClickHouse 26.8" and a real-time market data walkthrough on 4 September, scheduled upgrades for Managed Postgres on 3 September, MySQL CDC for ClickPipes reaching general availability on 2 September, and the RunReveal team welcomed on 1 September — the security-data direction that implies has still produced no product announcement. Managed ClickStack reached general availability at Open House in late May 2026, priced on infrastructure rather than events at under three cents per gigabyte per month for full-fidelity OpenTelemetry data and $0.002 per gigabyte per month for long-term retention; the pricing model, not the product, is the competitive threat. The ClickStack UI has shipped inside the ClickHouse binary since version 26.2, adding roughly 4.2MB and putting an observability UI on any install, though it omits alerting and persistence and is explicitly not for production. Financial backdrop: a $400 million Series D led by Dragoneer closed on 16 January 2026 at a $15 billion valuation, with annualised revenue past $250 million and ClickHouse Cloud past 4,000 customers as of the May 2026 disclosure. Near-term: Open House Bengaluru on 22 September, with the top three Click-a-Thon teams presenting on the main stage, and an On-Demand Compute webinar on 24 September after which the private preview begins rolling out.

New Relic

Quiet

Nothing was published in the 8–11 September window. The newsroom's most recent item remains the givestar announcement of 3 September: the UK charity-technology platform selected the Intelligent Observability Platform — APM, infrastructure monitoring and AI capabilities — for more than 750,000 registered users and over 250,000 non-profit organisations, replacing out-of-the-box Google Cloud monitoring. The displacement detail is the part worth keeping: New Relic is publishing wins against cloud-native monitoring defaults in mid-market and public-benefit segments rather than against other commercial platforms, which reads as defending volume at the entry tier while the enterprise contest plays out elsewhere. Consumption pricing and a 100GB monthly free tier are the mechanism. Product direction for 2026 is AI-lifecycle rather than infrastructure: Autopilot and Ground Truth were announced on 23 June alongside New Relic for Startups and a commitment to achieve FedRAMP High and DoD Impact Level 4 authorisations; AI Coding Observability launched on 8 June; a Kiro integration landed on 17 June; an SRE Agent launched at Advance 2026 in February; and the company was named a Leader in the 2026 IDC MarketScape for Worldwide AIOps in March. Hein Hellemons was announced as Chief Revenue Officer on 13 July, and the RUNTIME user conference at RenderATL was announced on 23 July. Federated Logs — querying logs across sources without moving them — is the platform capability most directly aimed at the same retention-cost objection ClickHouse and Splunk are both working, and it is the one to track. The Azure Marketplace co-sell relationship remains the channel most likely to appear opposite Elastic in Azure-committed accounts. Near-term events are EMEA workshops on Synthetics and on browser monitoring with session replay on 16 and 17 September, and o11y Hacks London on 30 September.

Splunk / Cisco

Quiet

Nothing was published in the 8–11 September window — an eighth consecutive silent cycle on the newsroom, which still shows no press release since 19 May 2026 and the $600 billion downtime research, with the most recent prior items dated 23 March on security for the agentic workforce and 4 March on TPG Telecom in Australia. The silence is inventory management: .conf26 opens in three days. Shipping has continued regardless — Splunk Enterprise Security 8.7.0 was released on 2 September 2026, with the Common Information Model add-on version-synchronised to 8.7.0 on the same day, and it remains the most substantive Splunk artifact of the past month. It moves the AI SOC Analyst from investigation to response by executing permitted actions automatically and recommending next steps from investigation context, and adds a Guided Response Agent, Automation Builder and Detection Builder agents, MCP access to investigation context, faster connector creation, refreshed navigation and deeper change history. Shipping an agent with permission to act, rather than one that only advises, is the claim the .conf26 security narrative will be built on. Running on a separate track is federated search in Splunk Cloud Platform, which queries Amazon S3 datasets — including those behind an Iceberg REST catalog — without ingesting the data; it is Cloud Platform only, unavailable for Splunk Enterprise on-premises or BYOL and absent from GCP, FedRAMP High and IL5 stacks, so the ingest-economics argument still lands in on-premises and regulated estates while in Splunk Cloud accounts the contest moves to query performance over cold object storage and total platform cost. The catalyst is imminent: .conf26 runs 14–17 September at the Colorado Convention Center in Denver, with the welcome keynote on Monday 14 September from 5:00 to 6:20pm MT — "Powering the rise of the agentic enterprise", presented by Cisco's Jeetu Patel and Splunk general manager Kamal Hathi with a guest — and a product keynote on Tuesday morning. The agenda is organised around three pillars: Platform as turning machine data into agentic action, Security as defending at machine speed, and Observability as optimising AI at scale. This is the last pulse before the announcements land; brief field teams on Cisco Data Fabric and federated-search positioning now, and expect the next two editions to be Splunk-dominated.
