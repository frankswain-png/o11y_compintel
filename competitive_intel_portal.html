Datadog Quiet

Nothing new published in the 5–8 September window — no product, partnership or analyst action. The insider-selling coverage that carried last cycle's entry, including the 5 September TipRanks item grouping Datadog with PDD, Roblox, Texas Instruments and Fastly, all reports transactions dated 1 and 2 September and is already logged in the previous edition.

The window's Datadog signal is therefore market rather than company: shares sit roughly 10% off recent highs while still up about 59% year to date, and early-September commentary attributes the pullback to a broad software and cybersecurity selloff, compounded by Snowflake's strong quarter and raised product-revenue guidance drawing attention to relative multiples. Nothing in that changes the competitive position; it does mean Datadog arrives at this week's investor appearances still needing to argue durability of AI-driven spend rather than expansion of it.

Standing items: Datadog presents at the Citi Global TMT Conference today, 8 September, at 11:30am ET, and at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology Conference on 10 September at 12:30pm ET — the first venues for updated AI-revenue commentary since the August guide. Datadog Summit San Francisco runs 23 September at the Hyatt Regency, a free one-day event on observability, security and AI. Product backdrop remains DASH 2026 in June, which shipped more than 100 capabilities including autonomous Bits AI, AI Guard, Bring Your Own Cloud, Bits Detection in preview and the Datadog Agent Console for tracking activity across Claude Code, Cursor and GitHub Copilot alongside Datadog's own agents; the company also acquired Adaptive ML. Sell-side targets have not followed the stock down — Baird moved to $300 from $210 after Q2, Needham to $300 from $260, Scotiabank to $285 from $275 — and consensus remains Buy across roughly 35 analysts. Open question is unchanged: whether the disclosed AI-customer usage reduction is a single renewal or the first of several.

Grafana Labs Quiet

Nothing published in the 5–8 September window. The only Grafana artifact dated inside it is the Weekly Community Recap of 7 September, which carries a recording of an AI Observability with Grafana talk by staff technical marketing manager Shawn Pitts, a list of September community events and a community-authored article on building an incident-response plugin — community programming, not a company development.

The nearest release is Grafana 13.2.1, shipped 2 September, a patch rather than a feature drop: it fixes CVE-2026-12704 and CVE-2026-14199 and corrects adhoc and groupby variable datasources on UI import, bundled plugins not moving correctly, and panel-editor options-pane resizing. Worth noting because a security-fix patch on a major version four months old is evidence of a maintenance cadence that enterprise buyers now ask about directly.

The commercial standing item is still the 26 August disclosure: 10,000 customers worldwide and more than $600 million ARR, against $400 million and 7,000 customers reported on 30 September 2025 — roughly 50% ARR growth in eleven months. Attach is the more competitively significant half, with average products per contracted customer nearly doubling in two years from 2.3 to 4.4, 87.8% of contracted customers on two or more products, 64.9% on four or more, and more than 18,000 organisations using Grafana Assistant. Product direction remains as set at GrafanaCON 2026 in Barcelona in April, where Grafana 13 launched alongside the Kafka-backed Loki ingestion rearchitecture, a new Loki query planner, Pyroscope 2.0, AI Observability in Grafana Cloud, the Grafana Cloud CLI for agent-driven workflows and o11y-bench, an open-source benchmark for evaluating AI agents doing observability work. Grafana Labs was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms and positioned furthest in Completeness of Vision, in the report published 13 July 2026. The Grafana Marketplace remains in pilot with Crest Data as a launch partner contributing ten plugins, and is still the open item worth tracking, since it is the mechanism by which Grafana converts its plugin ecosystem into a commercial distribution channel.

ClickHouse Active

Two posts landed inside the window, both on interfaces rather than observability features. "ClickHouse as a streaming HTTP API" by Mark Needham on 5 September makes the case for serving results straight over HTTP without an application tier in between. "How MCP Toolbox turns agent text into ClickHouse vectors" by Pete Hampton on 7 September walks end to end through Google's MCP Toolbox for Databases embedding agent text into vectors on both insert and search, leaving ClickHouse to rank the results and removing the need to build or operate an embedding service.

Read together with the previous cycle's output — the ClickStack-versus-Grafana decision guide on 5 September and the 87% cost-reduction Uken Games story on 1 September — the pattern is a vendor building the case that ClickHouse is where agents should read and write, then arguing observability follows from that rather than the reverse. This is a flanking route, not a frontal one: it never contests observability tooling on features, it makes the storage and retrieval decision first and treats the tooling choice as downstream. The MCP Toolbox post is the clearest instance yet, since it hands the vector-search step to an external Google component and keeps only the ranking, which is exactly the boundary ClickHouse wants buyers to accept as natural.

Watch signal: Elastic's strongest ground is that vector search, retrieval relevance and observability already sit in one platform with security alongside — the assembly ClickHouse is asking buyers to build from parts. Where these posts land, the counter is total operational cost of the assembled stack and the relevance quality of the retrieval layer, not a feature comparison against ClickStack.

Standing items: publishing cadence remains high, with "Pipelined SQL in ClickHouse 26.8" and a real-time market data app walkthrough on 4 September, scheduled upgrades for Managed Postgres on 3 September, and MySQL CDC for ClickPipes reaching general availability on 2 September. Managed ClickStack entered beta on 5 March 2026 and reached general availability at Open House in late May, priced on infrastructure rather than events at under three cents per gigabyte per month for full-fidelity OpenTelemetry data and $0.002 per gigabyte per month for long-term retention — the pricing model, not the product, is the competitive threat. The ClickStack UI has been embedded directly in the ClickHouse binary since version 26.2, adding roughly 4.2MB and making an observability UI available on any install, though it omits alerting and persistence and is explicitly not for production. RunReveal was welcomed on 1 September and the security-data direction it implies has still produced no product announcement. Financial backdrop: $400 million Series D led by Dragoneer closed 16 January 2026 at a $15 billion valuation, with annualised revenue reported past $250 million and ClickHouse Cloud past 4,000 customers as of the May disclosure. Open House Bengaluru runs 22 September, with Click-a-Thon finalists presenting. Open question is whether the Postgres, CDC, agent and observability tracks converge into a single stack pitch or stay separately sold.

Dynatrace Active

Dynatrace published its September community newsletter on 7 September. Two items in it are worth logging. The first is confirmation that the Dynatrace Community platform is migrating from Khoros to Discourse, with the opening targeted for the end of October 2026 and no firm date yet, bringing Markdown support and a built-in AI assistant. The second is the open-source dtwiz CLI, named topic of the month: it inspects a system, ranks its recommendations, then installs the matching ingestion method — OpenTelemetry Collector, OneAgent, the Kubernetes Operator, or AWS, Azure and GCP cloud integrations. Dynatrace describes dtwiz as early-stage and actively developed, with broader technology coverage and more languages for automatic OpenTelemetry instrumentation planned.

Neither is a product launch and neither will appear in a press release, which is why they are worth reading. dtwiz treats the OpenTelemetry Collector as a peer of OneAgent in the recommendation ranking rather than a fallback, from a vendor whose commercial advantage has historically rested on the proprietary agent. Combined with dtctl and dtmgd, it also signals that Dynatrace is investing in the command-line, as-code surface where platform engineering teams actually evaluate tools — the same audience Elastic reaches through Fleet, integrations and the Elastic Agent. The community replatforming matters for a duller reason: Discourse's AI assistant and open indexing make Dynatrace's accumulated troubleshooting corpus far more retrievable, including by the models buyers now ask before they ask a vendor.

Watch signal: Time-to-first-data is a live displacement axis and Dynatrace is automating it. Elastic's answer needs to be as fast and as opinionated in mixed estates where OpenTelemetry is already the ingestion decision — and the ground where Elastic can still take the argument is what happens after the data lands, on unified search, security and cost at retention volume rather than on the install step.

Standing items: the Arize acquisition remains the live corporate item and is not reported as closed. Dynatrace signed a definitive agreement on 13 August to acquire the AI observability vendor for approximately $915 million, about $815 million in cash plus replacement equity awards, expected to close later this quarter or early in Dynatrace's fiscal third quarter subject to regulatory review. Read alongside the 17 August private placement of $1.25 billion of zero-coupon exchangeable senior notes due 2031 — struck at an initial exchange price of roughly $64.27, a 35% premium to the $47.61 close, settling 20 August for about $1.227 billion net — this is a company financing an acquisitive posture rather than a defensive one. The most recent press release on the news centre remains "As AI scales across enterprises, breaking points emerge", dated 25 August. Dynatrace was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms for the sixteenth time, positioned highest for Ability to Execute, in the report published 13 July 2026. The company appears at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology Conference tomorrow, 9 September — the next likely venue for Arize close and integration commentary — and at WeAreDevelopers NA in San Jose from 23 to 25 September. Reported scale: approximately 4,100 global customers and more than 5,200 employees.

New Relic Quiet

Nothing published in the 5–8 September window, and the newsroom's most recent item remains the 3 September givestar announcement carried in the previous edition: the UK charity-technology platform selected the Intelligent Observability Platform — APM, infrastructure monitoring and AI capabilities — for more than 750,000 registered users and over 250,000 non-profit organisations, replacing out-of-the-box Google Cloud monitoring, with the stated goal of eliminating manual log searching.

The displacement detail in that release is the part worth keeping. New Relic is winning against cloud-native monitoring defaults rather than against other commercial platforms, in mid-market and public-benefit segments, and publishing named logos rather than platform news. That is consistent with a company defending volume at the entry tier while the enterprise contest plays out elsewhere. The consumption pricing model and a 100GB monthly free tier are the mechanism, and the reported base of roughly 175,000 organisations is the result.

Product direction for 2026 remains AI-lifecycle rather than infrastructure. New Relic Notebooks reached general availability on 11 August. In January the company launched an observability solution for ChatGPT Apps; at Advance 2026 in February it launched an SRE Agent under the framing of observability beyond human scale, alongside work tying technical performance to business outcomes; in June it launched New Relic for Startups with free platform access including every AI feature, plus AI Coding Observability aimed at AI-assisted development. It was named a Leader in the 2026 IDC MarketScape for Worldwide AIOps on 24 March. The Azure Marketplace co-sell relationship remains the channel most likely to appear opposite Elastic in Azure-committed accounts. EMEA workshops on Synthetics and on browser monitoring with session replay run 16 and 17 September, and o11y Hacks London is scheduled for 30 September.

Splunk / Cisco Quiet

Nothing published in the 5–8 September window — a sixth consecutive silent cycle on the newsroom, which still shows no press release since 19 May 2026, with the most recent 2026 releases dating to 23 March on security for the agentic workforce and 4 March on TPG Telecom in Australia. Shipping has not stopped: Splunk Enterprise Security 8.7.0 was released on 2 September, and it remains the most substantive Splunk artifact of the past month.

The 8.7.0 content matters more than the silence. The AI SOC Analyst and Guided Response Agent move from investigation to response, automatically executing permitted actions and recommending next steps from investigation context — the step from summarisation to action that most agentic security claims stop short of. The release also adds Automation and Detection Builder agents, MCP access to investigation context, refreshed navigation and change history. Shipping an agent with permission to act, rather than one that only advises, is the substantive claim Splunk will build the .conf26 security narrative on.

Running on a separate track, and not part of 8.7.0, is federated search in Splunk Cloud Platform, which lets teams query Amazon S3 datasets — including those backed by an Iceberg REST catalog, documented since around May 2026 — without ingesting the data. Splunk Federated Search for Snowflake, announced in 2025, is the same play against a different store. That capability is the more direct answer to the volume-pricing objection that drives displacement conversations, but it is Cloud Platform only: it is not available for Splunk Enterprise on-premises or BYOL, nor on GCP, FedRAMP High or IL5 stacks. The implication for Elastic is that the ingest-economics argument still lands in on-premises and regulated estates, while in Splunk Cloud accounts the contest moves to query performance over cold object storage, correlation quality and total platform cost.

Standing items: .conf26 runs 14–17 September at the Colorado Convention Center in Denver, six days out, with roughly 6,500 IT, security and data leaders expected, Cisco president and chief product officer Jeetu Patel among the keynote speakers, and an agenda built on agentic AI for the SOC, observability and the Splunk platform. The 2026 announcements should be read against what Cisco has already set up — the Cisco Data Fabric powered by the Splunk platform spanning Machine Data Lake and federated search, and Splunk Agent Observability powered by Galileo, shown at Cisco Live 2026 in Las Vegas. This is the last pulse before the announcements land; pre-brief field teams on Data Fabric and federated-search positioning now, and expect the next two editions to be Splunk-dominated.
